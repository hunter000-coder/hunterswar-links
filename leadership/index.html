1 · The foundation

What is leadership?

The vision says the word keeps changing (charismatic, servant, situational, transformational...), but three elements show up in every definition.

A process of ethically based influence
About achieving common objectives
Aims at the growth and success of individuals and the organisation
  • Influence, not coercion. Leadership is a two-way process between leader and group, built on role-modelling, inspiration, support, coaching and empowerment, "without coercion or pure power".
  • Results, with meaning. Leadership is a means to results achieved in a professional and ethical way. A leader explains why a goal matters.
  • Growth. Good leaders grow their people and themselves through feedback and self-reflection.

Four levels of leadership

Lead selfKnowing and mastering yourself: strengths, weaknesses, values and character.
Lead teamFostering trust, knowledge sharing and psychological safety.
Lead organisationLeading through others in dispersed structures by delegating and promoting a shared vision.
Lead strategicLeading at the highest echelons of risk, responsibility and complexity.

3 · KNOW → Defence values

Four core values

Respect, courage, integrity and loyalty shape identity and give cohesion and certainty. Both civilian and military personnel must consciously uphold them. When personal values clash with the organisation's, people can end up leaving.

Leaders show who they are through what they do. By not just knowing the values but embodying them, they build trust and a culture where people feel safe, recognised and engaged.

4 · KNOW → Competences

What a leader needs to know

A skill is the ability to perform an action or solve a problem. An attitude is how you approach a task. A competence combines knowledge, skills and attitudes, and it can be developed.

Professional competences

Technical skills specific to a role, field or profession, gained through education or experience. They vary by position and are essential for results and for keeping your team's trust.

Three kinds of skill

Technical: master the profession Human: collaborate with others Conceptual: see the bigger picture

Their importance varies with the level at which you lead. Three broader competences underpin them: problem-solving, social judgement and sound knowledge of your field.

Behavioural competences: five clusters

Leadership can be developed

Some people have natural talent for communication or negotiation and may progress faster, but those with less innate talent can become just as effective. Development happens through training, courses, evaluations, coaching and mentoring, and it is a collective effort, not a solo one. True leadership emerges "when people choose to follow the leader because they trust them."

5 · DO → Adapting style

Choosing your leadership style

Everyone has a preferred style, but effective leaders flex it without losing authenticity. Two dimensions matter: task orientation (goals, organisation, control) and relationship orientation (motivation, support, collaboration). Both rest on respect and mutual trust.

Three situational factors to read

  • Relationship with the group. Base authority on trust and competence, not just formal power.
  • Group characteristics. Analyse experience, competence and motivation, and give less confident people more guidance and experienced people more autonomy.
  • Nature and context of the task. Complex tasks call for a relationship-oriented style; routine tasks under time pressure call for a directive one.

The vision also asks leaders to consciously switch between leading, guiding, supporting and delegating, depending on who is in front of them and what the mission requires.

Inspire by leading by example

Leaders are followed for what they do, not what they say. Four principles help build a climate of mutual respect, development and operational readiness:

Idealised influence Inspiring motivation Intellectual stimulation Individualised consideration

That includes recognising and correcting your own mistakes.

Decisions, ethical dilemmas and communication
  • Decide. Analyse, draw conclusions, set clear objectives and make sure the team knows why they matter. Objectives only gain power when they are shared and embraced.
  • Account for it. Decisions may be made under time pressure or with incomplete information. You must be able to frame, justify and account for them, and show the moral courage to stand by them.
  • Ethical dilemmas. When values or norms conflict there is "no perfect solution without a moral cost". You act from your deepest convictions and Defence's core values, and carry the choice with integrity.
  • Communicate. Two-way communication with room for feedback reduces misunderstandings. Speak and write clearly, use body language consciously and listen with empathy.

6 · GROUP and CONTEXT

Followership and change

Leadership is not determined by the leader alone, but also by the group and context. The aim is to turn a collection of individuals into a cohesive team.

The courageous follower

Leading and following are inseparable, and within Defence a leader most often also plays the follower. Followership is "not a subordinate or less important role, but an essential counterpart to leadership". A good follower is proactive, self-reliant, disciplined and contributes actively to shared objectives.

  • View the leader and follower roles side by side. They involve different tasks and capabilities.
  • Train followers too, not only leaders.
  • Followers own their actions, but the leader ultimately keeps the final responsibility.
  • Create a climate where critical thinking is possible, so a courageous follower dares to say what could be done differently.
Cohesion, wellbeing and a learning group
  • Build cohesion by safeguarding the team's interests, creating trust and appreciation, setting clear rules against discrimination and racism, and mediating conflicts.
  • Leadership is not about popularity. A certain distance stays necessary to be objective and adaptable.
  • Protect physical and mental safety, prevent work-related incidents and take responsibility, accept residual risk and provide accountability.
  • Turn the team into a learning group where people develop their own and each other's competences.
  • Unity in diversity: feel responsible for every follower, whatever their race, gender, sexual orientation or identity, or religious belief, and use everyone's talents towards the common goal.
Leading through change: adaptive leadership

Change is a constant within Defence, and a fixed style does not suffice. In adaptive leadership the leader:

  1. Steps back to see the bigger picture.
  2. Identifies the challenges the group faces and how to tackle them.
  3. Manages tensions, creating enough calm and trust to keep functioning without letting discipline slip.
  4. Gives direction first to overcome resistance to change.
  5. Returns the work and responsibility to the team in good time, so people can develop their own solutions.

"A leader is one who knows the way, goes the way, and shows the way."

John C. Maxwell, quoted in the vision
